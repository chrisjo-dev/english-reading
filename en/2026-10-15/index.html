Would you get into a taxi that drives itself?
This week, Uber started testing self-driving cars on the streets of Manchester, with a safety driver behind the wheel and no passengers on board.
For now, the cars are only mapping the city's roads, but the company hopes to offer robotaxi rides in up to fifteen cities around the world by the end of this year.
The company says it wants to make this technology accessible to people across the country.
However, driver unions warn that thousands of jobs could be lost, and they are asking for help for the workers who would be affected.
Local transport officials, who have not yet received a single request to run driverless taxis in the area, say they will expect strong assurances about safety before these cars carry anyone on their roads.B2
